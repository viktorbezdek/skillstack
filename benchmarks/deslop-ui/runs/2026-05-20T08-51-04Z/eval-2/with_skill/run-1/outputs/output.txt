Both pieces have the same core problem: they talk about the product instead of helping the user. Here's a breakdown:

---

## Empty State

**What's wrong:**

| Pattern | Example | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| Hedging opener | "It looks like you haven't..." | The system *knows* — don't soften facts |
| Patronizing reassurance | "Don't worry" | Users aren't worried; you're creating anxiety that wasn't there |
| Passive CTA | "whenever you're ready!" | Removes urgency without giving a reason to act |
| Generic benefit list | "organize, collaborate, track" | True of every project tool ever made — says nothing |
| Exclamation marks | Two of them | Forced enthusiasm |

**Rewrite:**

> **No projects yet**
> Create your first project to start organizing your work.
> [Create project]

That's it. The button is the CTA — the copy doesn't need to repeat it or sell it.

---

## Onboarding Modal

**What's wrong:**

| Pattern | Example | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| Self-congratulation | "We've designed this to be intuitive..." | Nobody cares; show don't tell |
| Zero information | "see your important metrics" | Which metrics? What should they do first? |
| Corporate hedge | "Please don't hesitate to reach out" | Stiff and distant |
| No next step | Entire modal | Modal ends with no action — so why does it exist? |

**Rewrite:**

> **Here's what to do first**
> 1. Connect your data source — takes 2 minutes
> 2. Invite your team
> 3. Your first report auto-generates after your first sync
>
> [Get started] [Skip]

Or, if it's truly just orienting:

> **Your dashboard is ready**
> You'll see live metrics here once you connect a data source.
> [Connect now] [Explore first]

---

## The pattern to fix in both

**Before:** Talk about what the product can do, hedge everything, end passively.

**After:** State the situation plainly, give one clear next action, trust the user to click it.

Cut length by ~70%. Users scan empty states in under a second — every word past the CTA is noise.
