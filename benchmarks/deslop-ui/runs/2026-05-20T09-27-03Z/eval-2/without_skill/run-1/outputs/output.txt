## Empty State

**Problems:**

1. **"It looks like"** — the system knows exactly what's there. Hedging is false modesty.
2. **"Don't worry"** — nobody was worried. This creates anxiety it then dismisses.
3. **"easy and takes less than a minute"** — unearned promise. If it's easy, show them; don't announce it.
4. **The benefits list** — "organize work, collaborate, track progress" is generic marketing copy, not a reason to act *right now*.
5. **"whenever you're ready!"** — passive CTA with forced enthusiasm. Gives the user permission to do nothing.

**Suggested replacement:**
> No projects yet.
> 
> [Create your first project]

Or if context warrants a bit more:
> No projects yet. Create one to start organizing your work.
>
> [Create project]

## Onboarding Modal

**Problems:**

1. **"Welcome to your new dashboard!"** — filler opener. Every modal starts this way.
2. **"you'll be able to see all of your important metrics and data"** — says nothing specific. What metrics? Why does it matter?
3. **"We've designed this experience to be as intuitive and seamless as possible"** — self-congratulatory. The user will decide if it's intuitive. Claiming it signals insecurity.
4. **"so that you can focus on what matters most to you"** — meaningless. Every product says this.
5. **"Please don't hesitate to reach out"** — corporate filler with a hidden negative ("don't hesitate").
6. **No action** — the modal ends with a support offer, not a next step.

**Suggested replacement:**
> Here's what you can do from your dashboard: [specific thing 1], [specific thing 2], [specific thing 3].
>
> [Get started] · [Watch a 2-min intro]

---

**Pattern across both:** the copy talks about the product rather than what the user can accomplish. Cut the self-description and lead with the action.
