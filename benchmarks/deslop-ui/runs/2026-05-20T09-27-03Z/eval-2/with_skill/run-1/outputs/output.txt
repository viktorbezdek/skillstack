Both have the same core problem: they describe the experience instead of advancing it. Here's the breakdown:

---

## Empty State

**What's wrong:**

- "It looks like" — hedging. The system knows exactly what you have.
- "Don't worry" — condescending. Nobody was worried until you said that.
- "easy and takes less than a minute" — patronizing filler. Show, don't promise.
- The benefit list (organize, collaborate, track) is generic enough to apply to any SaaS product ever made.
- "whenever you're ready" — passive. Weakens the CTA right at the moment you need the user to act.

**Rewrite:**
> **No projects yet**
> Create one to start organizing your work.
> [Create project]

That's it. The button is the CTA — don't bury it under reassurance.

---

## Onboarding Modal

**What's wrong:**

- "This is where you'll be able to see all of your important metrics" — the user can *see* the dashboard. Describing it adds nothing.
- "We've designed this experience to be as intuitive and seamless as possible" — every product says this. It's a claim, not information.
- "focus on what matters most to you" — meaningless. Matters most to *whom* doing *what*?
- "Please don't hesitate to reach out" — textbook AI-slop phrase. Cut it.
- The entire modal contains zero actionable information.

**Rewrite:**
> **Your dashboard**
> Add your first metric to get started, or explore the [quick tour].

If you need a support line, make it a plain link: "Questions? [Chat with us]"

---

## The pattern to fix across both

These read like someone optimizing for *seeming* helpful rather than *being* helpful. The fix is always the same: **state what's true, then give one clear next action.** Cut everything in between.
