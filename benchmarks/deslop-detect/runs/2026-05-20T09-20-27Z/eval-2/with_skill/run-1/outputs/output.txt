## What Each Detector Measures

**GPTZero** uses two primary signals:
- **Perplexity** — how predictable each word choice is. AI picks the statistically likely next token. Humans don't.
- **Burstiness** — how much sentence lengths vary. AI clusters in the 15–22 word range. Human writing bursts: 3-word punches followed by 50-word sprawls.

**Originality.ai** runs a trained classifier on structural patterns — sentence frames, transition phrases, grammatical repetition. Vocabulary swaps don't move it. Synonym-spinning is useless against it. Only structural rewriting works.

## Rewrite

> Permanently isn't too strong a word. Companies like GitLab, Dropbox, and Airbnb didn't restructure around remote work because flexibility sounded appealing — they did it because the engineers and designers they most needed stopped accepting fixed-location terms, and once that happened, every inherited assumption about what a productive team had to look like came up for renegotiation. Of course, dynamics shifted. So did expectations. The part that catches most executives off guard isn't logistical — a Slack message sent from Berlin at 9 AM reaches Auckland before lunch; that's manageable — it's cultural: organizations that never questioned co-location are now being asked to justify it, and many are finding they can't. And those are, invariably, the ones losing their best people.
