## What Each Detector Looks For

**GPTZero** targets two primary signals:
- **Perplexity** — how predictable each word is given the surrounding context. AI generates the most statistically likely next token; humans don't.
- **Burstiness** — variance in sentence length. AI produces uniformly medium-length sentences (15–25 words). Humans burst: short punchy ones followed by long winding ones.

**Originality.ai** runs a trained classifier, not just perplexity math. It looks at:
- Sentence frame patterns (how sentences are structurally built)
- Transitional phrase fingerprints
- Opening structure homogeneity

Synonym spinning fools neither. Both detectors see through surface vocabulary changes because the structural fingerprint doesn't change.

## Why Your Intro Fails

The original has four problems stacked together:

1. **Zero burstiness** — all four sentences are 15–21 words. Completely flat variance.
2. **Identical sentence openings** — every sentence opens with a noun-subject (Remote work / Companies / This shift / Organizations). Classic AI structural pattern.
3. **Predictable vocabulary** — "fundamentally transformed," "attract and retain top talent," "higher employee satisfaction and productivity" are phrases with near-zero perplexity. The model chose the most expected words at every turn.
4. **No specificity** — no named companies, no numbers, no real-world anchors. Specificity raises perplexity by forcing rare tokens.

---

## Rewrite Strategy Applied

1. **Burstiness**: inject a ≤5-word sentence and a ≥35-word sentence; break the uniform 15–21 word pattern
2. **Sentence openings**: first opener must not be a noun-subject; vary all five grammatically
3. **Perplexity**: replace generic claims with named companies (Shopify, GitLab), a specific figure, an unexpected word choice
4. **Voice**: add a first-person editorial moment and a hedge that signals genuine uncertainty
5. **Structure**: one sentence runs long with subordinate clauses before landing; one lands in three words
