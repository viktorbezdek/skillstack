Here's a rewrite:

---

We started because we kept seeing the same problem: companies burning time on tools that weren't built for how they actually work.

So we built something different. Not a platform. Not a suite. A focused piece of software that does one thing well — [specific thing] — and gets out of your way.

We're a small team. Most of us came from [industry]. We've done the job you're trying to do, which means we know which parts are genuinely hard and which parts are just badly designed. That shapes every decision we make.

We're based in [city]. We answer our own support tickets. We don't have a roadmap driven by investor slide decks.

If that sounds like the kind of company you want to work with, [get in touch / try it free / see how it works].

---

**What I changed and why:**

- Opened with a specific problem instead of a self-compliment
- Replaced "passionate dedicated professionals" with actual personality
- Cut all the abstract nouns (excellence, transformation, holistic) — they say nothing
- Made the team real by giving them a background and a point of view
- Ended with a direct call to action instead of a vision statement

The bracketed parts are placeholders — the more specific you can make those, the stronger it gets. A company that makes accounting software for restaurants reads very differently than one that does API tooling for developers. Specificity is the whole game here.
